First, the mental model correction: it's not a websocket, and Pino's worker threads have nothing to do with reaching Grafana. Those threads are a purely local implementation detail — how Pino formats/writes without blocking your app — they never open a network connection anywhere.

A key clarification hiding in your question: "Grafana" and "the thing that stores your logs" are two different pieces of software. Grafana itself is just the dashboard/query UI — it doesn't ingest or store logs on its own. The log database sitting behind it is almost always Loki (same company, separate product; Grafana can also point at Elasticsearch or others, but Loki is the standard pairing). That distinction is exactly why your "do I need a port/hostname" instinct is worth double-checking — the answer depends on which of the two boxes below your app is actually the one it's supposed to talk to, and in the standard setup, the answer is neither.

Walking the diagram: your app writes plain JSON lines to stdout — that's it, that's the entire app-side job. Docker automatically captures whatever a container writes to stdout/stderr; this happens for every container, nothing to configure. A separate agent process (Promtail, or Grafana's newer unified agent, Alloy) runs alongside your containers, reads those captured logs off disk, and pushes them to Loki over HTTP. Loki stores and indexes them. Grafana queries Loki when you open a dashboard and search.

So: no, you don't open a port or configure a hostname in your NestJS app — that's the entire point of this pattern. Contrast with Redis: ioredis holds open a persistent socket that your app actively manages as a client. Here, your app doesn't know Grafana or Loki exist at all. All the connection details (Loki's address, how often to ship, what to tag) live entirely in the log shipper's config file and Grafana's data-source settings — files your devops team owns, not your codebase.

There is a second pattern where your app would need a host/port: a Pino transport called pino-loki that pushes straight to Loki's HTTP push API from inside your Node process, skipping the shipper agent. It's a real, supported option — just less common, because it couples your app to the logging backend directly (what happens to your requests if Loki is briefly unreachable?) and it's extra complexity most teams push down to the infra layer instead.

Since your devops team owns this decision, the one precise question worth asking them is: "Does my app just need to log to stdout, or do you want me to push logs somewhere directly — and if so, what's the endpoint?" Their answer tells you immediately which of the two pictures above applies. My bet, based on how you described it ("this project will use Grafana"), is the stdout pattern — which, conveniently, means the recommendation from my review a minute ago (skip the file target, log to stdout only) is the right call either way: it's exactly what the shipper-agent pattern needs, and it's a two-line change later if they turn out to want direct push instead.
